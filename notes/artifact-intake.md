tools against SAMR with a task, a reflection and a live link.

### Verdict: not artifacts, and nothing is lost

Discussion board posts are excluded by the Week 2 rules. That settles all three.

It costs nothing, because the content is already inside an artifact that is assigned. The Canvas
export holds **HPTT 803 Mini Project and Video compilation worksheet**, rated DELIVERABLE and
described as *twelve mini projects across traditional, online and hybrid settings plus video
projects: each with tool, SAMR level, live URL and reflection.* Four tools times three settings is
the twelve. These posts are the whole set.

The worksheet is the assembled artifact and already sits at **objective 6, slot A**. The posts are
its raw material, so there is no need to look for more of them.

### What they are worth keeping for

The reflections carry judgments specific enough that they would not survive paraphrase, and the
reflection field on the objective 6 page can use them directly:

- Diffit caps its reading level at Grade 11, below a medical student audience, and its open-ended
  prompts drifted toward personal growth rather than liver anatomy. MagicSchool AI, given the same
  task, does expose a graduate level and produced clinically applicable questions. Two tools, one
  task, opposite results — that comparison is the strongest thing in the three posts.
- Kahoot's free tier caps answers at four choices, which is the stated reason for not adopting it.
- Quiz Gecko needed a premium trial for the AI generation, named as the barrier to real use.
- Coggle was judged *modification* rather than *redefinition* on the explicit reasoning that the
  same result was achievable without technology — a rating argued down rather than up.
- The Gemini Gem cannot be shared, so students cannot reach the thing that was built.

The Gemini screenshots are the strongest single piece: a custom Gem playing *Jim Smith*, a
jaundiced patient in the emergency room, answering history questions in character. A working AI
standardised patient, and the screenshots are its only durable record.

One citation appears in post 3 and belongs in the artifact's reference list:
Misra, A. C., & Emamaullee, J. (2022). CAQ Corner: Surgical evaluation for liver transplantation.
*Liver Transplantation, 28*(12), 1936–1943. https://doi.org/10.1002/lt.26505

### The Padlet link should not be published as-is

`padlet.com/fpradhan/emet-introduction-704zq97pwi9jhyt1` is an introductions board, and post 2 says
it is intended for the new EMET medical students this autumn. If students have posted to it, a
public link on the portfolio exposes their names and writing to anyone who finds the site.
Screenshot the board empty, or with your own entry only, and publish that instead.

### Two links are edit links

Both the MagicSchool Google Doc and the Canva design end in `/edit`. If either is shared loosely, a
visitor can alter the work being offered as evidence. Canva has a *view* share link; the Google Doc
should be exported to PDF and hosted here.

### Nothing external was verifiable from here

This container's outbound access is an allowlist, so none of the links could be opened. Each needs
checking in a browser private window to confirm a stranger can reach it.

Kahoot, JeopardyLabs, Padlet, Coggle, Quizlet, ThingLink and Quiz Gecko are all third-party hosts,
several on free tiers. A portfolio meant to outlive the programme should carry its own copy — a PDF
or screen capture in `assets/artifacts/` — with the live link as a secondary reference.
