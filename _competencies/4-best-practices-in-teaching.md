## Integration Paper

**Course:** HPTT 801 – Foundations of Health Professions Education
{: .meta}

### Reflection

For the Integration Paper in HPTT 801 – Foundations of Health Professions Education, I revisited
the learning theory paper I had written earlier in the semester and accounted for where it
diverged from the curriculum I had actually built. I selected it to represent objective 4a because
it is the artifact where I audit my own design, which effectively demonstrates reflective
practice.

My original paper was written with medical trainees who had completed medical school, and
gastroenterology fellows rotating with the inpatient Hepatology team, in mind, where bedside
examination and endoscopy make modeling and enactive learning straightforward. As the semester
progressed, I pivoted to a fifteen-week curriculum aimed at medical students, which changed the
content, the means of assessment, and the location of instruction.

The most important part of the paper discusses the need to adjust learning theory to match the
change in curriculum while still maintaining elements of Bandura's theory. Weekly open-book
multiple-choice questions do not fit social cognitive theory well, and are better described by
Vygotsky's zone of proximal development and his sociocultural theory of language, since an
open-book policy lets students scaffold using textbooks, online resources, or each other. I
considered removing the open-book policy and using a locked browser, which would have aligned the
assessment more closely with Bandura's triadic reciprocal causation, but chose to retain open-book
because it mirrors how certification examinations and real clinical lookup function. One of the
challenges encountered was accepting that fidelity to real practice sometimes outranks theoretical
tidiness. Following this course, I now have a better understanding regarding the importance of
planning theory, activity, and assessment when building educational content rather than adapting
after the fact.

## Applying Science of Learning Activity

**Course:** HPTT 802 – Instructional Design for Health Professions Education
{: .meta}

### Reflection

The purpose of this assignment in HPTT 802 – Instructional Design for Health Professions Education
was to choose three of the twelve tips in Gooding, Mann and Armstrong's paper on the science of
learning in health professions education, find two published examples of each tip in practice, and
briefly summarize each example. I selected it to represent objective 4a because it required me to
find the evidence behind teaching practices I had been using.

My three tips came from three different themes. From applying learning to new and varied contexts,
I took the tip on preparing learners to transfer knowledge to new settings, pairing it with a
validation study for a colonoscopy simulation assessment tool and a study of virtual reality
endoscopic skill in subjects with and without prior gaming console use. From harnessing the power
of emotion, I took the tip on teaching learners to recognize their emotional state, pairing it
with a qualitative study of Schwartz rounds and a literature review on the role of emotion in the
transfer of clinical skills. From learning in social contexts, I took the tip on the social nature
of learning, matched with a randomized trial of multimedia tweets and a comparison of altmetric
scores and traditional published literature dissemination measures.

One of the challenges encountered was finding literature specific to hepatology. I had to think
more broadly to consider my gastroenterology learners and to look at literature outside my primary
specialty. The virtual reality skill study I found in the surgical endoscopy literature shaped the
ergonomics proposal I built later in HPTT 824 – Simulated and Virtual Learning in Health
Professions Education, where virtual reality develops foundational technique and augmented reality
reinforces it in the live endoscopy suite.
