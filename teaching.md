## Educational philosophy

Being an academic physician requires adaptability, medical expertise, and compassion. As a
Transplant Hepatologist I have the privilege of working with medical trainees at every stage
of the career — from pre-med through to Transplant Hepatology fellowship. I am a firm believer
in combining bedside education with real cases linked to key topics in hepatology, bridging the
gap between theoretical knowledge and clinical practice. My training across Canada, Ireland, and
the United States gives me both a diverse experience to draw from and the ability to connect with
students from a wide range of backgrounds.

By first understanding a trainee's baseline knowledge, I adjust a complex concept — chronic liver
disease, or liver transplantation — to engage them within their individual zone of development
(Pratt & Collins). My specific goals vary with a learner's stage of training, but in general I aim
to impart a better understanding of hepatic pathophysiology: not only to cover what standardized
testing assesses, but to improve patient outcomes and healthcare delivery.

Trainee learning can be assessed through several means. On an inpatient service I follow fellows'
progress through the day-to-day management of patients on the Hepatology service. More
longitudinally, I track knowledge and identify gaps through standardized testing such as the annual
in-training examination. Gaps are addressed individually through one-on-one feedback, or at group
level by adding lectures to the trainee curriculum. Where deficits are shared across a cohort, I
adjust the curriculum accordingly.

In short: I integrate bedside clinical medicine to augment education for trainees at all levels,
leveraging clinical expertise, an international background, and communication to improve trainees'
practice and support them through standardized examinations.
